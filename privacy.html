Gyan — Privacy Policy

Last updated 15 September 2026

Who runs Gyan

Gyan is a private messaging app made and operated by Gyandeep Mittal, an individual developer in Mumbai, India. There is no company behind it and no investors. Questions or requests: gyandeepmittal@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Gyan does not sell your data, and never will.
  • There are no adverts and no advertising or analytics trackers in the app.
  • Nothing about you is shared with other companies except the service providers listed below, which are needed to make the app work.
  • Location is city-level only, is switched off unless you turn it on, and no coordinates ever leave your phone.
  • You can ask for your account and everything in it to be deleted, and it will be.

Gyan does not collect your contacts, your browsing, your precise location, your advertising ID, or anything about what you do in other apps.

Calls

Voice and video calls are sent directly between the two phones wherever possible. When a direct connection cannot be made — common on mobile networks — the audio and video are passed through a relay server operated by Metered. That relay cannot see or hear the contents: calls are encrypted between the two phones, and the relay only forwards the encrypted traffic. Call audio and video are never recorded or stored. Only the fact that a call happened, who it was between, and how long it lasted are kept, so your call history works.

The AI assistant

Gyan includes an AI assistant you can chat with. When you send it a message, that message and the recent messages in that conversation are sent to Anthropic so a reply can be generated, and the reply is saved in your chat like any other message.

Only conversations that include the AI are sent. Your chats with other people are never sent to Anthropic. If you would rather not use it, simply do not message it.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Camera — to take photos and videos to send, and for video calls. Only when you open the camera.
  • Microphone — for voice notes, video snaps and calls. Only while recording or on a call.
  • Photos — so you can choose a picture you already have.
  • Approximate location — only if you switch on the usage map, and only to work out your city name on the phone.
  • Notifications — to tell you about messages and calls.

You can refuse or withdraw any of these in Android Settings. The rest of the app keeps working.

How long things are kept

  • Disappearing snaps are deleted automatically once viewed or once they expire.
  • Messages, posts and media stay until you delete them or your account is deleted.
  • Error reports are kept while the fault is being fixed, and cleared afterwards.
  • Deleting your account removes your profile, messages, media and posts.

Your choices

  • See or correct your information — most of it is in the app under Settings.
  • Delete your account and data — email gyandeepmittal@gmail.com from your account's email address and it will be done, normally within 30 days.
  • Get a copy of your data — ask at the same address.
  • Turn the usage map off — in the app; your city is removed.

Children

Gyan is not intended for children under 13, and accounts are not knowingly created for them. If you believe a child has an account, write to the address above and it will be removed.

Security, honestly stated

Traffic between the app and its servers is encrypted, and the database restricts every table so that people can only read their own information and what has been shared with them. Calls are encrypted between phones.

Gyan is made by one person, not a security team. Messages are not end-to-end encrypted: they are stored on the server and could in principle be read by the operator. Please keep that in mind and do not use Gyan for anything you would be harmed by having exposed.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version appears on this page. For anything significant, you will be told in the app.
